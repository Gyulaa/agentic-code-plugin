## Google's people-first questions
From Google's guidance on helpful content; a page should answer yes:
- Does it give original information, research or analysis, and go beyond the obvious?
- Does it cover the topic thoroughly, and add value beyond the sources it draws on?
- Is the title descriptive rather than sensational? Would someone bookmark or share it?
- Does it show first-hand experience and real expertise? Are there easily checked factual errors?
- Would the audience find it useful if they came directly, not from search? Will they leave having reached their goal?
- **Who** wrote it (visible, with background), **how** was it made (including any automation), **why** does it exist (to help people, not to attract search traffic)?
Experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust (E-E-A-T) are not a direct ranking factor, but trust is what the system looks for, and the bar is higher on topics that affect health, money or safety.

## Structure an answer engine can quote
- Headings phrased as the questions people ask; under each, the answer in the first one or two sentences, then the detail, the conditions and the examples.
- One topic per page, covered with sub-sections for the related questions. AI Overviews and AI Mode split a question into many sub-searches ("query fan-out"); a thorough page answers several of them. A page per phrasing is scaled content, not a strategy.
- Facts a machine can lift without the context: numbers with units, prices with the date they apply, named sources, definitions in one sentence. Tables for comparisons, numbered lists for steps.
- Everything important as text in the HTML: not only inside images, tabs that load on click, or JavaScript-only widgets.

## What the evidence on AI citations says
| Finding | Strength | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Adding quotations, statistics and cited sources raised visibility in generative answers by up to about 40% on a research benchmark; keyword stuffing lowered it (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) | one study, older engines, its own metric | verifiable specifics help readers anyway; don't promise a number |
| AI search relies far more on third-party sources than on brand-owned pages, unlike Google's more balanced mix (Chen et al., 2025) | large controlled comparison | reputation is built off the site too |
| Branded web mentions correlate with AI Overview visibility much more than backlinks do (Ahrefs, 75,000 brands, 2025) | correlation | the same direction |
| Structured data showed no measurable effect on ChatGPT citations in a controlled test of 1,885 pages (Ahrefs, 2026) | controlled | write schema for rich results, not for AI |
| `llms.txt` is not used by the major AI systems (Google's Mueller and Illyes, 2025; server logs) | statements and logs | not by default |

## Trust signals
- A named author with a short bio and a link to more (profile page, publications); for a business, the people behind it.
- An About page, a real address and contact options, legal pages, clear prices or how pricing works.
- Published and updated dates; sources linked; corrections visible.
- Own photos and own data rather than stock and borrowed numbers. Invented credentials or AI personas destroy trust and break Google's rules.

## Page types
| Page | What it needs |
|---|---|
| Home | what the business is, for whom, where; links to the main sections |
| Service or product | one per service or product; what it is, who it is for, price or how pricing works, proof, the next step; Product or Service details visible |
| Article or guide | a question worth answering, the answer first, the author, the date, sources |
| Comparison or alternatives | honest criteria, including where the other option is better |
| Location page | only with real local content (team, projects, opening hours, directions); never one template copied city by city |

## Local businesses
- Google's local ranking rests on relevance, distance and prominence. A complete, current Google Business Profile (categories, hours, photos, services), and the Bing equivalent.
- The same name, address and phone number everywhere (site, profiles, directories).
- Real reviews, asked for honestly, answered, never bought or filtered; `LocalBusiness` structured data matching the profile.

## AI-written content
- Allowed by Google when it helps people; producing many pages with AI and no added value breaks the scaled content abuse policy, and quality raters are told to rate such pages lowest.
- A person fact-checks everything before publishing, including metadata, alt text and structured data.
- Product data generated by AI is labelled as such in Google Merchant Center; AI-generated images carry the metadata it requires.

## Measurement
- Google Search Console: indexing, queries, pages, Core Web Vitals; AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in the "Web" totals.
- Bing Webmaster Tools: search performance and the AI performance report (Copilot and Bing AI citations, the queries behind them).
- Analytics: a custom channel group with a regex for AI referrers (for example `chatgpt\.com|perplexity\.ai|gemini\.google\.com|copilot\.microsoft\.com|claude\.ai`); many AI clicks still arrive as "Direct". Analytics tools that set cookies or process personal data go through `privacy-check`.
